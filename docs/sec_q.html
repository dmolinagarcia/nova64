Q

Decision log · open questions

what was decided, why · what's left to closeSHEET 17

The decisions shaping the current design, with their reasoning in one line — and the honest list of what's still open.

  1. D01
    ECP5 → 3× iCE40 TQ144 — BGA isn't hand-solderable; 0.5mm TQFP is. Accepted consequence: 128 Kbit EBR → HW walker + small TLB instead of on-chip tables.
  2. D02
    superseded by D13/D15 Main PSRAM removed; the 32 MB SDRAM is the memory — the QSPI gateware (the trickiest part) disappears, and a cache miss drops from ~µs to ~200–300 ns. The removal stands and now covers the whole board, but its headline no longer holds: with the SRAM narrowed to 8 bits a fill is back in the µs range (D15). The decision survives on the gateware simplification alone.
  3. D03
    superseded by D15 External table SRAM removed; a single 2 MB SRAM (cache + tables + pinned) — ~35 pins that didn't fit in the TQ144.
  4. D04
    Amiga-style shared bus; out goes the dedicated FPGA-to-FPGA link and the eDP bridge — Neon taps the bus with the $FE window.
  5. D05
    Direct RGB-TTL 10.1” panel (ER-TFT101-1) — no bridge chip, no NDA (RTD2556 ruled out due to Realtek firmware). eDP/ANX6345 remains a future variant. This choice is size-bound, and that is the thing to know about it. Parallel RGB-TTL exists only up to roughly 10 inches; from 14 inches upward every panel is a laptop panel speaking LVDS or eDP, and the direct path is simply gone (→ Q23).
  6. D06
    superseded by D13 512×300 → 1024×600 px-doubling — the framebuffer fits within the video PSRAM's budget and its QSPI bandwidth. With the PSRAM gone and 64 MB behind Neon, capacity is no longer the constraint. Px-doubling may still be right on panel bandwidth or CPU cost, but that argument has not been made — pending with the pin recount (→ Q12).
  7. D07
    USB-only HID (RP2040's PIO-USB) — out go PS/2 and keyboard matrix. Capacitive GT911 touch: the resistive option needed 4 ADCs the RP2040 doesn't have free.
  8. D08
    SD as the only boot path; dedicated BIOS flash removed — incompatible with the pin budget after adding the SDRAM.
  9. D09
    Boosts (backlight, host VBUS) hanging off SYS, not VBAT — the machine runs on USB with the battery depleted. VPP 2.5V via BAT54: out goes the dedicated rail.
  10. D10
    Full 2 KB paging per process (out goes the "one bank per process" model) — a tractable 32 KB flat table, decoupled from the cache's 256 B fill granularity.
  11. D11
    Hardware walker instead of a software trap on TLB miss — with 10 ns SRAM it costs ≈100 ns and simplifies the kernel.
  12. D12
    32-bit PTE with a 16-bit frame from day one — 128 MB ceiling with no format migrations. 16 rather than the 15 bits the pool needs is free: the flags occupy exactly 16 bits, so the other half-word was going unused, and the clean split lets the walker read 32 bits and take the low half as address and the high half as permissions with no shifting or masking (→ sheet K).
  13. D13
    PSRAM eliminated from the entire board, Neon's framebuffer included — every QSPI interface disappears with it. Neon gets its own 64 MB SDRAM instead: same part, same footprint, same controller as Helium's. Streaming pixels across a link would need ~26 MB/s sustained with hard deadlines and would make Helium the bottleneck for the whole machine. A second SDRAM device costs the same pins as any smaller memory and buys double buffering at 16 bpp.
  14. D14
    SDRAM sized at 64 MB per device (AS4C32M16SB-7TIN, 512 Mbit) — the ceiling of SDR silicon in volume; no 1 Gbit part exists. The 256 Mbit W9825G6KH stays as a drop-in fallback on the same footprint. The second device is deliberately not populated: at 8 MHz the 65816 sustains ~4 MB/s, so merely zeroing 64 MB takes ~16 s. A second /CS is routed to an empty footprint.
  15. D15
    SRAM narrowed to 1 MB ×8 with D0–D7 and A0–A10 shared with the CPU nets — 13 pins instead of 41, which is what makes the budget close at 90/107. Possible only because A0–A10 are the untranslated page offset (→ F.2). Accepted costs: Helium can no longer reach the SRAM alone, walks cost four accesses instead of two, and the shared nets carry three loads.
  16. D16
    Cache misses handled by stalling PHI2 low, not by ABORT — the W65C816S core is fully static and the WDC datasheet sanctions holding the clock indefinitely. ABORTB is retained for genuine protection faults only, which removes the frequent case from the most delicate path on the chip. Clock gating was also preferred over the RDY pin for a specific reason: RDY is reported to be ignored during write cycles on some revisions, a 6502 NMOS inheritance. Gating the clock sidesteps the question — with PHI2 stopped the CPU is not in any state at all, rather than in one whose rules depend on the cycle type.
  17. D17
    The SRAM is a hardware cache with tags, never a tier of allocatable frames — migrating a page would force hardware to rewrite PTEs, which mshare and TLB coherence make impossible (→ L.3). 768 KB of 4-way cache in 2 KB lines with 256 B sub-blocks, plus 256 KB fixed and uncached.

Open questions

  1. Q1
    blocking ER-TFT101-1 module datasheet: backlight current/voltage, strap exposure on the FPC, GT911 connector. Order the panel in the capacitive variant. Blocks the KiCad schematic for sheets 1.5/1.6.
  2. Q2
    HDL choice (Verilog vs VHDL) — constrains the reference softcore (P65816 is VHDL).
  3. Q3
    Specific SDRAM controller among the three open-source candidates (adaptation and verification in E3).
  4. Q4
    EC↔kernel protocol: transport and event format for HID, battery, RTC, and shutdown.
  5. Q5
    Exact format of the BIOS → kernel info block.
  6. Q6
    Backlight brightness control: PWM from the RP2040 (simpler) or from Neon.
  7. Q7
    contradiction Is the inter-FPGA link back? The revised pin budget reserves 8 pins for one and reasons about feeding video across it, but D04 removed it and B.3 has Neon tapping the shared bus. Either D04 is being reversed in silence or those 8 pins belong to something else.
  8. Q8
    blocking The 17 free pins on Helium do not cover SPI-SD, the UART console and the debug port. Allocated today: inter-FPGA link 8, I2C 2, RP2040 handshake 3, spare 4. Sheets D and F both depend on interfaces that have no pins — the budget does not close as written.
  9. Q9
    How many page tables actually stay resident? The 256 KB fixed region must hold the kernel (64 KB), the vectors, bank $00 and the active tables at 32 KB each. K.2 used to promise 16 residents out of 512 KB; the honest figure now looks closer to four.
  10. Q10
    SRAM footprint compatibility: confirm in the datasheets that A20 on the 2 MB AS7C316096 lands on an NC pin of the 1 MB AS7C38096A in TSOP-II 44. Usual for Alliance, unverified.
  11. Q11
    SDRAM column count: confirm the 512 Mbit part uses 10 column bits against the 256 Mbit part's 9, with the pinout otherwise identical — the premise the fallback footprint rests on.
  12. Q12
    Neon's pin budget with its own SDRAM needs a proper recount; the preliminary estimate was 100 of 107 (panel 22 at 18 bpp, VGA R-2R 14, I2S 4, SDRAM 39, link 16, config 4, backlight PWM 1). A 24 bpp panel would reach ~106, so 18 bpp is the recommendation. The replacement rationale for px-doubling (→ D06) belongs here too.
  13. Q13
    Is the softcore even the right lever? Given that memory and not the core is what caps this machine (→ E.10), effort may be better spent on the cache and memory subsystem than on Argon. Worth deciding explicitly before anything is invested in FPGA-C.
  14. Q14
    Argon's role and memory path — two halves of one question. Does the softcore replace the physical W65C816S on the shared bus or coexist with it? And if populated, does it use the standard bus into Helium, with the same PHI2 and cache constraints as the physical chip, or a dedicated wider link into Helium's cache? Without the latter its advantage is marginal. Related to Q7: both ask what private FPGA-to-FPGA paths this board actually has.
  15. Q15
    The real iCE40 Fmax of a candidate core: synthesize P65816 stripped of its SNES bus-timing fidelity on the target part and read the placed-and-routed figure. Every speed estimate in sheet E is guesswork until this number exists.
  16. Q16
    Base core: P65816 cleaned up and given a flat memory interface, for maximum compatibility, versus FT816 for speed first — pending an instruction-coverage audit of FT816. Tied to Q2, since P65816 is VHDL.
  17. Q17
    Freeze the canonical user virtual map within $02–$FD: where .text ends and .data begins, stack size, heap start. The bank boundaries themselves are settled and not part of this question — an early draft placing user .text at $01:0000 was rejected against L.10.
  18. Q18
    Bank $00 — settled in principle: each process's stack pages are pinned for its whole lifetime, and the vectors, trampolines and kernel stack occupy one or two privileged pages mapped identically in every process (→ L.11). What is left is sizing: the exact split of those one or two pages between the three tenants, how large the kernel stack should be, and whether there is one shared kernel stack or one per process.
  19. Q19
    Audit Calypsi's calling convention and its direct-page pseudo-register usage. Two things fall out of it: the exact context-switch save set, and how syscall arguments are passed.
  20. Q20
    Specify the binary format header — field layout, magic value, versioning.
  21. Q21
    Shared read-only text: is libc's .text duplicated in every process, or mapped shared read-only across page tables? The PTE R/X bits already allow it and the memory win is free, but it puts page reference counting into the loader.
  22. Q22
    Bank $FF register map — Helium's block is specified (→ sheet M). Still unassigned: the timer and interrupt controller, the peripheral block, and Neon's registers. Keep the 256-byte walls between blocks so the decode and the privilege check stay trivial.
  23. Q23
    What a panel larger than 10.1” would cost. RGB-TTL dies above ~10”, so a 14” class panel means a transmitter bridge — an ANX6345, taking the parallel RGB Neon already produces and running eDP link training itself in hardware, configured over I2C by the RP2040 before the CPU leaves reset. On iCE40 that is effectively the only route: driving LVDS needs a 7:1 output serializer and native eDP needs hard SerDes, and neither exists on this family — so the two alternatives an ECP5 would have had are both closed here. Knock-on costs: ~22 pins on Neon at 18 bpp against a budget already near its limit (→ Q12), a framebuffer and bandwidth budget to re-derive at the new resolution, a larger backlight boost than the PT4110 sizing in C.6, and touch to re-specify — laptop panels are usually non-touch, which puts the GT911 of D07 in question.
  24. Q24
    ASID width, still unfixed. It drives both EBR consumption per TLB entry and how often an ASID must be recycled — and every recycle carries a mandatory flush (→ M.8). Four bits (16 contexts) and six (64) have both been floated; validate against the EBR budget alongside the 32–64 TLB entries already planned.
  25. Q25
    How the RP2040 reaches Helium's control registers with the 65816 held in reset — reusing the config SPI path, or genuine bus mastering. Unresolved, and it decides what the E2 stage can actually test.
  26. Q26
    How big is the EC's flash, and does the machine boot without a card? This document says W25Q16 (2 MB), firmware only, with the SD as the sole boot path (D08). The schematic sheets specify a W25Q128 (16 MB) holding backup copies of all three bitstreams and the BIOS — which would let the machine cold-boot with no card in the slot. That is a capability, not a BOM line, and it has to be decided before sheet 1.2 is drawn.
