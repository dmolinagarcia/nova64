E

CPU — W65C816S and the softcore option

the part · the pins the design leans on · what actually caps speedSHEET 05

The 65816 is the premise of the project, not a component choice open for review — the lineage argument is in A.3. This sheet gathers what the rest of the design assumes about it, and settles what an FPGA softcore in Argon's slot would and would not buy — a question worth closing before anyone populates that footprint.

  1. E.1
    The part: W65C816S in a PLCC-44 socket, running in 24-bit native mode (CLC/XCE at reset). WDC grades the silicon from 1 to 14 MHz; this machine targets 8 MHz at 3.3V, with PHI2 generated by Helium. The 14 MHz figure is a property of the chip, not a ceiling anyone here is pushing against — the limit that binds is memory (→ sheet F).
  2. E.2
    The programmer's model the whole system rests on: 16-bit accumulator and index registers with widths switched at runtime by the M and X flags, the E flag for 6502 emulation mode, 24-bit addressing split into 64 KB banks via DBR and PBR, a relocatable direct page, and a stack that always lives in bank $00 — which is why that bank is pinned (→ L.10).
  3. E.3
    Four pins carry more architectural weight here than is usual. ABORTB cancels an instruction with no side effects, and is what turns page permissions into real protection. VPA/VDA distinguish an instruction fetch from a data access, which is what makes the X permission checkable at all. BE tristates the CPU's buses — used by Argon, and far more often by Helium to take the shared nets during a fill. RDY freezes the CPU, and is bidirectional: the CPU pulls it low on WAI.
  4. E.4
    The core is fully static: WDC's datasheet sanctions holding PHI2 in either state indefinitely as a standby mode, with no minimum clock frequency. That property is not a curiosity — it is what licenses the cache-miss stall (→ D16), and it makes a stopped clock the machine's low-power mode for free.
  5. E.5
    Argon (FPGA-C) holds an unpopulated softcore slot that takes the same bus with BE=0. The point of the slot is optionality — it lets the physical chip be replaced without redesigning anything around it. Its 3V3 and 1V2 rails sit behind their own 0Ω jumpers, so an empty footprint cannot load the bus. Giving the softcore its own device rather than a corner of Helium was deliberate twice over: it hands Helium back the block RAM and routing the memory subsystem needs, and it lets the core's clock be tuned without dragging MMU timing along with it. Whether populating it is worth doing is the rest of this sheet.
  6. E.6
    What a softcore would have to preserve is narrower than it looks: only programmer-visible compatibility. The 92 opcodes and every addressing mode, the E and M/X flags, the full status register, and the interrupt vectors and entry semantics. Microarchitecture, pipelining, cycle counts and the external bus protocol are all free to change — and nearly all the available speed lives in what can be thrown away, starting with the faithful reproduction of the original multiplexed bus dance and its dummy reads.
  7. E.7
    Two bottlenecks that must not be conflated. Core Fmax: the critical path runs through microcode ROM → ALU → address calculation; registering the microcode ROM output is free if it lives in EBR, and the decimal-mode adder is a habitual offender. Cycles per instruction: the original spends 2–7 cycles, many of them wasted on the physical bus. CPI is the larger prize and the cheaper one — it buys throughput without touching the critical path.
  8. E.8
    Reality check on this platform. The often-quoted 80–100 MHz and "20–30× an original 3 MHz machine" are figures for mid-range parts (Artix-7, Cyclone V) and do not transfer to iCE40, where complex logic realistically tops out near 50 MHz. A full microcoded 65816 is complex logic; expect the low tens of MHz. open No number here should be believed until a candidate core has been placed and routed on the target part (→ Q15).
  9. E.9
    The "run from block RAM" path that makes softcores fast elsewhere is closed on iCE40: 128 Kbit of EBR total, ~16 KB, already shared with the TLB and the cache tags. There is no room to execute a meaningful program on-chip.
  10. E.10
    Which leaves the real conclusion: the ceiling is memory, not the core. Argon would ride the same shared bus, the same Helium cache and the same PHI2 window as the physical chip, so its effective speed is gated by the identical hierarchy. A softcore can win on CPI, on added features, or on deleting a PLCC part from the BOM — not on raw clock, and only if given a wider or more direct path into Helium's cache than the multiplexed external bus allows. No such path exists in the design today (→ Q14).
  11. E.11
    Honest caveat: the 65816 is an accumulator architecture — memory-traffic-heavy and fairly serial, with little instruction-level parallelism to exploit. Even pipelined, data dependencies cap the gains. "The fastest possible 65816" is a well-defined goal and a bounded one; it will not scale the way a modern core does.
