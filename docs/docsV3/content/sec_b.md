# Global architecture
> physical CPU + optional softcore · Helium = northbridge · Neon = video/audio

An Amiga-style shared bus with four inhabitants: the physical CPU, two always-present FPGAs — Helium (FPGA-A) and Neon (FPGA-B) — and an optional third, Argon (FPGA-C). The RP2040 stays off the bus, acting as the board controller.

- B.1 — CPU: W65C816S in a PLCC-44 socket, 24-bit native mode. PHI2 generated by FPGA-A (Helium), target 8 MHz at 3.3V. The BE signal lets the CPU release the bus — used both by Argon and, far more often, by Helium itself to take the shared nets during a fill (→ [F.4](sec_f#f4)). Detail in [sheet E](sec_e).
- B.2 — FPGA-A (Helium) — the "northbridge": MMU + TLB + walker, cache, bus arbiter, prioritized interrupt controller, system timer, UART, SPI-SD, I2C, the debug agent (→ [sheet R](sec_r)), and CPU glue logic. **A pure state machine**, never a soft CPU: anything in the critical memory path pays for itself on every access. Intelligence — filesystems, protocol stacks — lives in the RP2040 or in 65816 software.
  NOTE: iCE40HX8K TQ144 · budget: 90/107 pins for CPU bus + SDRAM + SRAM, 17 free. [[open]] the free pins do not yet cover SPI-SD, UART and the debug port (→ [Q8](sec_q#q8)).
- B.3 — FPGA-B (Neon) — video and audio: taps the bus directly; the CPU reaches its **data** through a 64 KB VRAM window in bank `$FE` and its **registers** through a 256-byte window at `$FF:8000`, both validated by the MMU's NEON_BUS_SEL signal. Its own 64 MB SDRAM (chip RAM — same part and same controller as Helium's), RGB-TTL output to the panel, bring-up VGA, and PCM5102A over I2S. **Fully specified in [sheet T](sec_t)**: three video modes, a blitter, and a command processor that renders whole frames from a list in SDRAM with the CPU doing nothing. **Audio is [sheet W](sec_w)** — four channels mixed in hardware, because the 65816 has 181 cycles per sample frame and cannot mix anything ([W.1](sec_w#w1)).
  NOTE: Local memory rather than streaming pixels from Helium: video across a link would need ~26 MB/s sustained with hard deadlines and would make Helium the bottleneck for the whole machine.
  NOTE: **Neon is the one subsystem on this board that is useful before anything else works.** Its text mode lives entirely in the FPGA's own block RAM, initialised from the bitstream, and depends on no SDRAM, no arbiter and no bus: a correct screen exists the moment `CDONE` rises, with Helium unconfigured and the 65816 still in reset (→ [T.1](sec_t#t1), [T.53](sec_t#t53)). That ordering is deliberate and should be preserved as the design evolves.
- B.4 — FPGA-C (Argon) — optional, unpopulated at first: a 65816 softcore (P65816 reference) that takes the same bus with BE=0. Allows switching from the real CPU to a softcore without touching the rest.
  NOTE: Whether that is worth doing is a live question — on this platform the win is CPI and features, not clock (→ [sheet E](sec_e), [Q13](sec_q#q13)).
- B.5 — RP2040 — embedded controller off the bus: configures, boots, and watches over the system (→ sheet D). The board's only mandatory flash: its W25Q16.
- B.6 — Interconnect: a single shared A/D/control bus. The dedicated *data* link between FPGAs was removed, but **three** control lines survive it, and the naming says what they are for: **we select Neon's bus, and while Neon's bus is busy Helium holds the CPU.**

| Net | Direction | Asserted means | Idle / pulled state |
|---|---|---|---|
| `NEON_BUS_SEL` | Helium → Neon | "Helium has validated this cycle for you" — for `$FE`, after translation and the permission check; for `$FF:8000`–`$FF:80FF`, after the bank decode and the privilege check | **Deasserted.** Active low with a pull-up, so an unconfigured Helium cannot select Neon by accident ([B.8](sec_b#b8)) |
| `NEON_BUS_BSY` | Neon → Helium | "I cannot serve this cycle yet." Its meaningful edge is the **deassertion**, which says the data is in Neon's output register ([T.18](sec_t#t18)) | **Asserted.** Active high with a pull-up — the one place where the pulled state is the *asserted* one, and deliberately so |
| `NEON_IRQ` | Neon → Helium's PIC | Vblank, raster compare, or a display list's `SIGNAL`; aggregated inside Neon and demultiplexed by the kernel reading `IRQ_STATUS` ([T.45](sec_t#t45)) | Deasserted |

  NOTE: **`NEON_BUS_SEL` qualifies two windows, not one**, and Neon needs no extra pin for the second: it already taps A0–A15 and already captures the bank byte off D0–D7 during PHI2 low, so recognising bank `$FF` as well as `$FE` is the same comparator with a different constant (→ [T.21](sec_t#t21), [D36](sec_q#d36)).
  NOTE: **`NEON_BUS_BSY` stalls PHI2, not RDY.** [D16](sec_q#d16) rejected RDY for cache stalls because some W65C816S revisions ignore it during writes, and the same argument applies here; gating the clock sidesteps the question entirely and Helium already owns that gate. It is worth more than it looks — it is the reason Neon's graphics aperture can be *read* at all, which deleted a whole diagnostic register path REV A carried (→ [T.7](sec_t#t7), [D32](sec_q#d32), [D37](sec_q#d37)).
  NOTE: **Busy-by-default is a safety property.** Were the net idle-low, Helium could stall on a read and immediately sample "not busy" before Neon had noticed the cycle at all, then resume onto a bus nobody was driving. It also makes an **absent, unconfigured or reset Neon read as busy**, so the machine stalls and Helium's watchdog reports it rather than returning whatever a floating bus settles at — which matters because [E1.4](sec_p#e14)'s staged-population rule guarantees stages where Neon is not fitted (→ [F.11](sec_f#f11)).
  NOTE: **These three nets are the whole of the inter-device contract, and that is what the prototype board has to emulate.** [Sheet P](sec_p)'s carrier merges Helium and Neon into one ECP5, where the temptation is to let them talk through anything convenient — an earlier proposal for the carrier invented a 56-signal command and peek link for exactly that reason, unaware that [D04](sec_q#d04) had already deleted it. On one die `neon.v` still takes A0–A15, D0–D7, PHI2, RWB and `NEON_BUS_SEL` and still drives `NEON_BUS_BSY` and `NEON_IRQ`, and nothing else crosses ([P.13](sec_p#p13)). **A merged die that respects this table is a re-partitioning; one that does not is a rewrite.**
  NOTE: **Those three nets now have a second tenant, and the contract was written in a way that allows exactly that.** [Sheet X](sec_x) gives an expansion slot `/EXP_SEL_n`, `/EXP_BSY` and `/EXP_IRQ` — the same shape, the same stall-first protocol, the same watchdog — with one polarity deliberately reversed: a slot's busy line idles *not busy*, because an empty slot must not stall a machine the way an unfitted Neon deliberately does ([X.11](sec_x#x11)). **The inter-FPGA contract itself is untouched; what generalises is its shape**, which is what [Q56](sec_q#q56) chose the names for.
- B.7 — Clocking: **one 25 MHz oscillator** in a star to the three FPGAs, **33 Ω in series in each branch**, entering each through a GBIN pin; every device's PLL then synthesises what it needs — Helium's core and PHI2, Neon's pixel clock, Argon's core. One part, one net, and no cross-chip skew to reason about — and the series resistors are not optional decoration: an unterminated star into three CMOS inputs rings, and a clock that rings is a clock that double-triggers.
  NOTE: **This is what Neon's specification got wrong at REV A**, and it is the one board-level correction sheet T had to make: it derived a single 102.000 MHz domain from a 12 MHz crystal, which does not exist on this board and is not reachable from 25 MHz. The nearest legal PLL setting is **103.125 MHz core, 51.5625 MHz pixel**, and the vertical total moves from 635 to 640 to land back at ~60 Hz (→ [T.4](sec_t#t4), [Q43](sec_q#q43)). Neon's second PLL is then unspent and has two claimants, audio and the VGA bring-up clock (→ [T.9](sec_t#t9)).
  NOTE: The star gains a fourth destination the moment a slot exists. [X.15](sec_x#x15) puts the 25 MHz reference on the connector with its own series resistor — and makes a fan-out buffer the answer to a second slot, rather than a fifth branch hung on the same net.
- B.8 — Safe state with no bitstream loaded: **RESB pulled down** so the CPU stays in reset, control lines pulled up so nothing reads as asserted, and the shared data bus held by weak pull-ups so it never floats.
  NOTE: The board can be powered with all three FPGAs blank and nothing fights for the bus — which is exactly what makes the staged bring-up of [E1.5–E1.7](sec_p) possible.
  NOTE: **"Control lines pulled up" hides one exception that is electrical rather than logical.** `RDY` is not an input: it is **bidirectional and open-drain**, and the CPU pulls it low itself while executing `WAI`. So it needs a markedly stronger pull-up than the rest — of the order of 3.3 kΩ against 10 kΩ — or the rising edge is slow and a `WAI` takes its time releasing. Every other control net is driven push-pull by exactly one device and its pull-up is only there for the unconfigured case.
  NOTE: **`NEON_BUS_BSY` is the one documented exception to "nothing reads as asserted", and it honours the rule's intent rather than its letter.** For that net the safe state *is* the asserted one: a blank or absent Neon reading as busy makes Helium stall and its watchdog report a fault, where reading as not-busy would make Helium resume onto an undriven bus and hand the CPU whatever the pull-ups settle at. Both nets still carry pull-ups; what differs is which state the pull-up means ([B.6](sec_b#b6), [T.18](sec_t#t18)).
