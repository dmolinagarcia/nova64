# Vision and philosophy
> what it is · what for · under what rules · how to read it

Project framework: what is being built, what finishing it means, what principles govern every decision, and how the rest of these sheets are meant to be used.

- A.1 — What it is: an original portable personal computer built around the W65C816S, designed from scratch as a learning exercise in electronics, PCB design, gateware, computer architecture, and OS design. **From scratch is meant literally**: the schematic, the board, the FPGA gateware, the BIOS, the kernel and the windowing system are all written here. What is bought is bought as a part and not as a solution — a CPU, three FPGAs, a microcontroller, memories, a panel and a DAC — and everything that turns them into a machine is ours.
  NOTE: **The learning is the deliverable, and it settles the build-or-buy calls.** A subsystem that could be finished faster by dropping in someone else's block, but would teach nothing on the way, is designed here rather than imported — and where the trade was made the other way it is recorded as a decision in [sheet Q](sec_q).
- A.2 — The counterfactual it answers: **the 16-bit machine Commodore never built.** Had it not bought Amiga and gone looking elsewhere for a 16-bit future, the obvious move was to follow its own 8-bit line onto the 6502's natural successor. noVa64 is that machine — the Commodore that would have come next.
  NOTE: **It is a compass, not a costume.** The lineage is inherited in character — a shared bus, custom chips beside the CPU, video and audio doing the work the processor cannot — and not in the bill of materials: those custom chips are FPGAs. The question asked of a decision is what that engineering culture would have done with parts a hobbyist can buy and solder today, not what 1987 happened to stock.
- A.3 — Which settles why the CPU is a 65816 and not something better behaved. Lineage first: it is where the 6502 family goes. Then the practical case — 6502 simplicity kept, 24-bit addressing, 16-bit registers, and, decisively, the **ABORT** pin. ABORT is what separates a machine that can protect memory from one that merely divides it (→ [sheet E](sec_e)).
  NOTE: The CPU is the premise, not a choice still under review; what *is* open is whether a softcore ever stands in for the physical part — the second half of [sheet E](sec_e), resting on a footprint that ships unpopulated and on the pin list of [E.13](sec_e#e13).
- A.4 — Inspiration and goals: two milestones define success. **Apple II Milestone** — the machine boots on its own to a prompt. **Amiga Milestone** — preemptive multitasking with a windowed GUI.
  NOTE: The second is named for the capability, not the lineage: it is the bar the Amiga set, cleared by the machine Commodore would have built instead of buying it.
  NOTE: Both have an exact position in the build rather than a mood: the first closes with stage **E6** — SD, a minimal kernel and a serial shell — and the second with **E8**, the panel, the battery and the compositor. Everything in [sheet P](sec_p) is either on the path to one of them or explicitly marked as being off it.
- A.5 — **Compatible with nothing.** This machine runs its own software; it is not an emulator and no existing binary is expected to work on it. That is a freedom, not a gap — it is what removes any need for cycle-exact behaviour, and therefore what licenses a cache with variable latency, a stalled clock, and a soft core that need not match the silicon cycle for cycle. Several later decisions rest on this one and would collapse without it.
  NOTE: **The freedom is paid for once, in full.** Nothing above the transistors exists yet, so the BIOS, the filesystem, the scheduler, the drivers, the toolchain and the GUI are all inside the scope of this project — which is what the whole Software area is for. It also removes the last court of appeal: when a sheet asks whether some behaviour is *correct*, the answer can only come from this document and from the tests it sets, because there is no reference machine to defer to and no existing program whose expectations have to be met.
- A.6 — Non-negotiable principles: every component hand-solderable (TQFP / PLCC / TSOP / 0.5 mm max.), everything at 3.3V, open toolchain (KiCad · Yosys · nextpnr · IceStorm · pico-sdk), incremental progress with an explicit verification criterion per stage, and unpopulated footprints to preserve future expansion.
  NOTE: Each of those has already cost something and been paid. Hand-solderable is why the FPGAs are three iCE40 in TQ144 rather than one BGA, and the small block RAM that follows is why the MMU walks its tables in hardware ([D01](sec_q#d01)). An open toolchain rules out any part whose use depends on an NDA or a vendor blob, which is what removed a panel-bridge candidate outright ([D05](sec_q#d05)). One rail keeps every level shifter off the board, and an unpopulated footprint costs pennies at fabrication and is unbuyable afterwards.
- A.7 — **Scope of this document: it is the specification, not an index to one.** It began as an outline tying together detail that lived in separate design documents, and for a while the two had to be read side by side. That is over — every one of those documents has been folded in, and where they disagreed with this board the reconciliation is recorded here rather than left for the reader to perform. **Nothing in this project is documented anywhere else**, and a claim that cannot be found in these sheets should be treated as not having been made.
  NOTE: The converse is the working rule: a decision taken but not written down is not a decision, and when the board and a sheet disagree one of the two is a bug — which one gets settled in [sheet Q](sec_q) before anything is cut.

## How to read this document — ten areas, twenty-four sheets, and a letter that never moves.

The sheets are grouped into ten **areas**, and the order of the areas is the reading order: Introduction, Architecture, Power, Embedded control and boot, CPU and system, Memory and storage, Video + audio, Software, Project and build, and the Appendix. The sheet *number* follows that order. **The sheet *letter* does not, and that is deliberate.**

- A.8 — **The letter is the sheet's address, not its position.** Letters were handed out in the order the sheets were written, and every cross-reference in the prose points at one — [sheet T](sec_t) for Neon, [T.53](sec_t#t53) for a single item inside it. Renumbering them to match the areas would rewrite several hundred references at once and break every link anyone has kept, so it is not done: a new sheet still takes the next free letter and is placed in whichever area it belongs to.
- A.9 — **Every item is numbered and individually addressable.** `F.4` is the fourth item of sheet F, and it can be linked to directly; the reference `(→ [Q41](sec_q#q41))` means an open question is tracked in [sheet Q](sec_q) and this item depends on how it closes. Items are the unit the document argues in — a sheet is a container, an item is a claim.
- A.10 — **A gold `TEST ▸` line is a verification criterion**, not commentary: it says what has to be observed before the item is considered true. A dimmed line below an item is a note — context, a caveat, or the reason a decision moved.
- A.11 — **Chips mark the state of a question.** A mint chip [[open]] is something undecided but not in the way; a gold chip [[!blocking]] is a contradiction or a dependency that stops work until it closes. Both are resolved in [sheet Q](sec_q), which holds the decision log and the open questions with their numbers.
- A.12 — **The checkboxes belong to [sheet P](sec_p)** and only to it: that sheet is the build, and its boxes carry the four states below. The gold banner marks a milestone from [A.4](sec_a#a4).
  NOTE: Pending, done, in progress, optional. **Those boxes are the only claim this document makes about what physically exists**; every other sheet describes the design as specified, whether or not anyone has yet soldered it.
- A.13 — **The twelve figures are numbered across the whole document**, not per sheet, and each one lives in the sheet it illustrates. [Z2](sec_z2) lists all twelve with their sheet, and [Z1](sec_z1) is the glossary for every acronym used anywhere — deliberately redundant, since each acronym is also expanded where it is first used.
- A.14 — **Conventions in the prose.** An address is written `$FF:8000` when the bank matters and flat, `$FE0000`, when it is being used as an operand; register and signal names are in `code` font. **Polarity is stated, never inferred from a name** — the idle and asserted states of the inter-device nets are given explicitly in [B.6](sec_b#b6)'s table, because the naming across the board is not consistent enough to be read as a convention.
- A.15 — **Three ways in.** Front to back is one reading, and the areas are ordered for it. A hardware reading can stop after Memory and storage; a software one can start at the [BIOS](sec_i) and take everything below the kernel–hardware interface of [sheet M](sec_m) as given. Anyone who only wants to know what is being worked on right now should read [sheet P](sec_p) and [sheet Q](sec_q) and nothing else — between them they hold the build state and every question still open.
- A.16 — **One machine, two build tracks — and every sheet specifies the target board.** [Sheet P](sec_p) runs a **P-series** prototype on a commercial Colorlight i9 module, with Helium and Neon merged into a single ECP5 and the CPU, SRAM, EC and audio outside it, beside the **E-series** that builds the three-iCE40 board this document describes. The prototype is temporary packaging of the same design, not a variant of it ([D40](sec_q#d40)): where the two differ — no battery, no charger, no panel, so sheets [C](sec_c) and [S](sec_s) go unexercised — the difference is a gap recorded in sheet P, never a second specification competing with the sheet it came from.
  NOTE: The revision line under the masthead is **per subsystem, not per document**: power, Neon, the GUI, the audio path and the prototype each revise on their own schedule, and the outline number moves independently of all of them. A superseded revision is not silently overwritten either — Neon REV A's single 102 MHz clock domain does not exist on this board, and the correction to 103.125 MHz lives where the reader meets the problem, in [B.7](sec_b#b7) and [T.4](sec_t#t4), with the reasoning in [sheet Q](sec_q).
