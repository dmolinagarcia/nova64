# Physical memory structure
> how CPU · FPGA-A (Helium) · SRAM · SDRAM relate

All system memory lives behind FPGA-A (Helium): the CPU never sees SRAM or SDRAM directly, only virtual addresses translated by the MMU and served by the cache. The SRAM sits on the CPU's own nets, the SDRAM behind the FPGA — an asymmetry that falls out of one property of the page size.

- F.1 — Hierarchy: registers → **SRAM cache** → **SDRAM** (physical frames) → microSD (swap, post-v1). The SRAM is a hardware cache with tags, **not** a tier of allocatable frames — see [L.3](sec_l#l3) for why that distinction is forced.
- F.2 — The key insight: with 2 KB pages, **A0–A10 are never translated** — the physical address is FRAME : offset, and the offset comes straight from the CPU. So both the data bus *and* the low address bus can be shared nets: the SRAM's address bus *is* the physical address bus, split at the page boundary, and Helium drives only the translated half.
  NOTE: D0–D7 → CPU net (0 pins) · A0–A10 → CPU net (0 pins) · A11–A19 → Helium (9) · /CE, /OE, /WE (3). Thirteen pins for 1 MB, against 41 for the ×16 part it replaces.
- F.3 — What makes the sharing work is the half-cycle pipeline. **PHI2 low**: the CPU drives A0–A15 and the bank byte on D0–D7; Helium latches the bank **internally — no external '573 is needed**, decodes it, and — if it is not `$FF` — forms the 24-bit address, does the TLB lookup (~10 ns in EBR) and prepares A11–A19 and /CE. **The bank decode comes first, before any translation or tag lookup**, so an `$FF` access short-circuits straight to the I/O bus: it never evicts a hot line to read a status register, and its latency is fixed. **PHI2 high**: /CE asserts and the SRAM has the whole high phase (~62 ns at 8 MHz) to deliver data straight to the CPU, with no FPGA in the data path.
  NOTE: Two dividends: ABORTB gets half a cycle of margin, since translation and permission checks finish before the rising edge; and the SDRAM leaves the critical timing path altogether.
- F.4 — Price of the shared nets: Helium **cannot reach the SRAM on its own**. Page-table walks, SDRAM→SRAM fills and the RP2040's BIOS preload all require taking the bus — RDY low to freeze the CPU, BE low to tristate it, then Helium drives A0–A10 and D0–D7 through pins it already has. No pin cost, real gateware cost.
  NOTE: Also: three loads (CPU, Helium, SRAM) now hang off D0–D7 and A0–A10. This is the most layout-critical net on the board.
- F.5 — SRAM 1 MB ×8 (AS7C38096A-10TIN, TSOP-II 44, 3.3V, industrial), partitioned: **256 KB fixed and uncached** — kernel, active page tables, bank `$00`, interrupt vectors; directly addressed, never evicted, and where the `PIN` PTE bit applies — plus **768 KB of cache**: 384 lines of 2 KB, **4-way set associative**, tags (~1.5 KB) in EBR beside the TLB.
  NOTE: 10 ns is mandatory: /OE must be deasserted while PHI2 is low, since the bus carries the bank byte then. Direct mapping was never an option — 32,768 physical lines competing for 384 positions is 85:1. [[open]] the 256 KB may not hold the resident tables K.2 promises (→ [Q9](sec_q#q9)).
- F.6 — Fill granularity: a whole 2 KB line over the 8-bit shared bus costs ~41 µs, plus SDRAM read, plus write-back of a dirty victim — 50–100 µs with interrupts blocked, which is unacceptable. Fills therefore move **256 B sub-blocks**, with valid and dirty bits per sub-block and one tag per 2 KB line: ~6 µs per fill, healthy interrupt latency, and the part of the page never touched is never transferred.
- F.7 — One consequence of sizing the tagged line at 2 KB: **a cache line is exactly a frame**, and both are 2 KB-aligned. So the "flush everything cached for frame N" operation the kernel needs before paging a frame out (→ [L.16](sec_l#l16)) collapses to a single tag lookup and up to eight dirty sub-block write-backs.
  NOTE: Helium must expose that operation. It is cheap now; when the line was 256 B the same job meant sweeping eight separate lines.
  NOTE: **And it must expose the same operation without the write-back**, for frames the SD engine is about to overwrite. Same tag lookup, same sizing argument, opposite treatment of the dirty bits (→ [M.12](sec_m#m12), [G.9](sec_g#g9)).
- F.8 — SDRAM 64 MB (AS4C32M16SB-7TIN, 32M×16, 512 Mbit, TSOP-II 54) — 32,768 frames of 2 KB, one device on Helium and an identical one on Neon. 512 Mbit is where SDR silicon tops out; no 1 Gbit part exists in volume.
  NOTE: The 256 Mbit W9825G6KH is a drop-in fallback: same footprint, same 13 address lines, one column-address bit fewer — a controller parameter, not a board change. Alternates: IS42S16320F-7TLI, IME5116SDBETG-75IA.
- F.9 — Off the critical path, the SDRAM controller stops being a low-latency backing store and becomes a **DMA and paging engine**. Substantial de-risking for bring-up: its timing no longer gates the CPU.
- F.10 — Architectural ceiling: 128 MB physical (16-bit frame in the PTE), **deliberately not populated**. A second device would double the RAM the 65816 can barely touch — at 8 MHz it sustains ~4 MB/s, so merely zeroing 64 MB takes ~16 s. A second /CS is routed to an unpopulated footprint and left there.
  NOTE: **The half that is not populated now has a claimant, and it is not memory.** [X.8](sec_x#x8) gives a range of those unreachable frame numbers to the expansion slots, so a PTE can name a card instead of the SDRAM with the TLB and the permission bits still in front of it. The ceiling stops being purely theoretical without a single frame of DRAM being added — and the frames spent that way are frames the machine could never have populated anyway.
- F.11 — Bring-up requirements that come with the stall (→ [L.4](sec_l#l4)): SDRAM auto-refresh must be interleaved into the fill state machine; a **watchdog** in Helium must resume PHI2 and raise NMI or ABORT if a fill overruns, since a hung fill freezes the machine with no clock and no diagnostic path; clock gating must be glitch-free and the first pulse on resume must meet the minimum PHI2 high width; RDY is bidirectional — the CPU pulls it low on `WAI` — so open-drain with pull-up, never driven hard high.
  NOTE: Free bonus: a stopped clock is literally the low-power mode.
  NOTE: **A third trigger arrives with the expansion slot.** A card that asserts `/EXP_BSY` and then dies stalls the clock exactly as an unfitted Neon does, so the same counter must resume PHI2, return `$00` and raise the fault — with a cause code naming the slot, since "something timed out" is not a diagnosis when the something is user-installed ([X.12](sec_x#x12)). One timeout, three clients, still no number ([Q57](sec_q#q57)).
  NOTE: **That watchdog now covers a second client, and it is what makes Neon's read path safe to build.** A CPU read of the `$FE` aperture stops PHI2 until Neon deasserts `NEON_BUS_BSY` ([T.18](sec_t#t18)); if no answer arrives inside the window, Helium must resume the clock, return `$FF` and raise the fault exactly as it does for an overrunning fill. Without it, a Neon that is unconfigured, held in reset, or **simply not fitted yet** — which [E1.4](sec_p#e14)'s staged population guarantees — freezes the machine with no clock and no way to say why. Same timer, one more trigger, and the timeout value is still unassigned (→ [Q57](sec_q#q57)).
- F.12 — Neon's 64 MB = **chip RAM** (framebuffers, image atlases, pre-rendered level buffers, command lists and audio DMA buffers), outside the CPU hierarchy and reached through the `$FE` window. At 103.125 MHz on a 16-bit bus that is **206 MB/s peak, ~150 MB/s effective**, of which scanout takes under 3 % — so **bandwidth is not a design constraint for Neon; logic is** (→ [T.13](sec_t#t13), [T.3](sec_t#t3)).
  NOTE: The PSRAM is gone from the whole board, and with it every QSPI interface.
  NOTE: **Neon has a second memory, and the division between them is strict.** Its 32 EBR blocks — 16 KB, fully allocated — hold everything that must be reachable with no wait state and everything scanout needs at single-cycle latency: the 8 KB text buffer, the 4 KB font, the palette, the scanout line buffers, the blitter's burst buffers and the command FIFO. The SDRAM holds everything large and nothing latency-critical. That split is what lets Mode 0 exist with the memory controller unwritten (→ [T.11](sec_t#t11)).
- F.13 — Layout note: SRAM and SDRAM in a short comb hugging Helium — the SDRAM side runs at ~100 MHz and the shared side has three loads; neither tolerates long stubs.
