# CPU — W65C816S and the softcore option
> the part · the pins the design leans on · what actually caps speed

The 65816 is the premise of the project, not a component choice open for review — the lineage argument is in [A.3](sec_a#a3). This sheet gathers what the rest of the design assumes about it, and settles what an FPGA softcore in Argon's slot would and would not buy — a question worth closing before anyone populates that footprint.

- E.1 — The part: W65C816S in a PLCC-44 socket, in **24-bit native mode** (`CLC/XCE` at reset). WDC grades the silicon 1–14 MHz; this machine targets **8 MHz at 3.3V**, PHI2 from Helium. The 14 MHz is a property of the chip, not a ceiling anyone is pushing against — the limit that binds is memory (→ [sheet F](sec_f)).
- E.2 — The programmer's model the whole system rests on: 16-bit accumulator and index registers with widths switched at runtime by the **M and X flags**, the **E flag** for 6502 emulation mode, 24-bit addressing split into 64 KB banks via **DBR** and **PBR**, a relocatable direct page, and a stack that always lives in bank `$00` — that bank is pinned (→ [L.10](sec_l#l10)).
- E.3 — Four pins carry more architectural weight here than is usual. **ABORTB** cancels an instruction with no side effects, turning page permissions into real protection. **VPA/VDA** distinguish an instruction fetch from a data access, which is what makes the `X` permission checkable. **BE** tristates the CPU's buses — used by Argon, and far more often by Helium to take the shared nets during a fill. **RDY** freezes the CPU, and is bidirectional: the CPU pulls it low on `WAI`.
  NOTE: Three consequences of those four that are easy to miss and expensive to discover. **`RDY` is an I/O in Helium, not an input** — the arbiter and the debug halt logic have to treat it as one, or `WAI` never releases ([E.13](sec_e#e13)). **Every chip select must be qualified with `VDA` ∨ `VPA`**, because with both low the cycle is internal and the bank bits are don't-care ([F.3](sec_f#f3)). And **`M`/`X` are invalid during the cycle after `REP`, `SEP` or `PLP`** — anything sampling them to size an access has to skip that cycle.
  NOTE: `ABORTB` is the pin with the most conditions attached, and they are the subject of [E.14](sec_e#e14) onward.
- E.4 — The core is **fully static**: WDC's datasheet sanctions holding PHI2 in either state indefinitely as a standby mode, with no minimum clock frequency. That property is not a curiosity — it is what licenses the cache-miss stall (→ [D16](sec_q#d16)), and it makes a stopped clock the machine's low-power mode for free.
  NOTE: The same property makes the debug agent's indefinite halt legal ([R.14](sec_r#r14))
- E.5 — Argon (FPGA-C) holds an unpopulated softcore slot that takes the same bus with BE=0. The point of the slot is optionality — it lets the physical chip be replaced without redesigning anything around it. Its 3V3 and 1V2 rails sit behind their own 0Ω jumpers, so an empty footprint cannot load the bus. ((Giving the softcore its own device rather than a corner of Helium was deliberate twice over: it hands Helium back the block RAM and routing the memory subsystem needs, and it lets the core's clock be tuned without dragging MMU timing along with it.)) Whether populating it is worth doing is the rest of this sheet.
- E.6 — What a softcore would have to preserve is narrower than it looks: only **programmer-visible compatibility**. The 92 opcodes and every addressing mode, the E and M/X flags, the full status register, and the interrupt vectors and entry semantics. Microarchitecture, pipelining, cycle counts and the external bus protocol are all free to change — and nearly all the available speed lives in what can be thrown away, starting with the faithful reproduction of the original multiplexed bus dance and its dummy reads.
- E.7 — Two bottlenecks that must not be conflated. **Core Fmax**: the critical path runs microcode ROM → ALU → address calculation; registering the ROM output is free in EBR, and the decimal-mode adder is a habitual offender. **CPI**: the original spends 2–7 cycles, many wasted on the physical bus.
  NOTE: CPI is the larger prize and the cheaper — throughput off the critical path.
- E.8 — Reality check on this platform. The often-quoted 80–100 MHz and "20–30× an original 3 MHz machine" are figures for mid-range parts (Artix-7, Cyclone V) and **do not transfer to iCE40**, where complex logic tops out near 50 MHz. A full microcoded 65816 is complex logic; expect the low tens of MHz. [[open]]
  NOTE: No number here should be believed until a candidate core has been placed and routed on the target part (→ [Q15](sec_q#q15)).
- E.9 — The "run from block RAM" path that makes softcores fast elsewhere is **closed on iCE40**: 128 Kbit of EBR total, ~16 KB, already shared with the TLB and the cache tags. There is no room to execute a meaningful program on-chip.
  NOTE: That budget is not merely small, it is committed: the TLB, the cache tags and the trace ring ([R.16](sec_r#r16)) spend the same 128 Kbit, and Argon would be asking after them.
- E.10 — Which leaves the real conclusion: **the ceiling is memory, not the core.** Argon would ride the same shared bus, the same Helium cache and the same PHI2 window as the physical chip, so its effective speed is gated by the identical hierarchy. A softcore can win on CPI, on added features, or on deleting a PLCC part from the BOM — **not on raw clock**, and only if given a wider or more direct path into Helium's cache than the multiplexed external bus allows.
  NOTE: No such path exists in the design today (→ [Q14](sec_q#q14)).
- E.11 — Honest caveat: the 65816 is an accumulator architecture — memory-traffic-heavy and fairly serial, with little instruction-level parallelism to exploit. Even pipelined, data dependencies cap the gains. "The fastest possible 65816" is a well-defined goal and a bounded one; it will not scale the way a modern core does.
- E.12 — **Three of the five native vectors are load-bearing here.** Entry pushes PBR, PC and P, clears `D`, sets `I`, forces `PBR = $00`, and vectors through bank `$00`: `$FFE4` COP, `$FFE6` BRK, `$FFE8` ABORT, `$FFEA` NMI, `$FFEE` IRQ. **COP** is the syscall entry ([M.1](sec_m#m1)), **ABORT** the page fault ([L.4](sec_l#l4)), **BRK** the debug breakpoint ([M.3](sec_m#m3)).
  NOTE: Those pushes are stack writes in bank `$00`, and an ABORT taken while entering the ABORT handler has nowhere to go — which is what pinning that bank ([L.11](sec_l#l11)) forecloses.
  NOTE: Five vectors in twelve bytes at the top of bank `$00`, `$FFEC` reserved. The rest of that bank belongs to the machine, not the CPU ([L.11](sec_l#l11)).
- E.13 — **What a softcore owes the board is a pin list, wider than [E.6](sec_e#e6)'s programmer-visible set.** `VPA`/`VDA` valid every cycle, or `X` cannot be checked; `ABORTB` honoured with side effects suppressed, the instruction restartable; `BE` releasing the buses; `RDY` bidirectional, or `WAI` never releases. And a fifth [E.3](sec_e#e3) omits: **`VPB`**, the privilege transition Helium reads on the vector fetch ([L.12](sec_l#l12)).
  NOTE: A cycle-exact core carries these because it reproduces everything; an accelerator such as FT816 is exactly where the audit below has to start.
  NOTE: A core that keeps those pins and changes everything else is still a drop-in.

## The ABORT pin — what it guarantees, and the four documented cases where it does not.

[E.3](sec_e#e3) names `ABORTB` as one of four pins carrying architectural weight, and [sheet L](sec_l) builds real memory protection on it. This section is the audit that claim needs, because **no fielded 65816 virtual-memory machine exists to copy from**, and because WDC's own datasheet enumerates the cases in which an abort modifies state rather than suppressing it.

- E.14 — **`ABORTB` has no timing parameter of its own.** It is governed by the generic *processor control* setup and hold times it shares with `IRQB`, `NMIB`, `RESB` and `RDY` — 10 ns each at the 5 V / 14 MHz grade, setup relaxing to 15 ns at the 3.3 V / 8 MHz grade this machine runs at. **Anyone citing a dedicated abort timing symbol is mistaken**; there is none.
  NOTE: **The budget is comfortable here and would not be at the top of the range.** A 14 MHz cycle is ~71 ns, so with setup and hold the MMU has roughly 35–50 ns after `VDA`/`VPA` and the address go valid to complete a TLB lookup, a permission check, and drive the pin — tight enough to argue for a registered TLB whose hit or miss is a single combinational term. At **8 MHz a cycle is 125 ns** ([E.1](sec_e#e1)), and the same decision has nearly twice the room. It is one more reason the clock target is where it is, and it should be re-derived rather than assumed if anyone ever raises it.
- E.15 — **The assertion rules are three, and all three are conditions on *when*, not on *how long*.** Assert only once `VDA` or `VPA` says the cycle is a real bus access — the two read `00` during internal dead cycles, and aborting one of those is meaningless. **Hold until `PHI2` falls, then release immediately**, and never hold longer than one cycle. And **never assert while `VPB` is low**: the pin is level-or-pulse sensitive, its internal latch clears on the second cycle of the abort sequence, and an abort asserted during the abort's own vector fetch aborts the abort ([E.18](sec_e#e18)).
- E.16 — **In the good case the sequence is register-preserving, and the one detail that makes demand paging possible is which address is stacked.** The instruction runs to completion with **no register or memory changed**; then `PB`, then the **aborting instruction's own address** — not the following one — then `SR`; `I` is set, `D` cleared, `PB` forced to `$00`, and `PC` loaded from the abort vector at `$00:FFE8` in native mode ([E.12](sec_e#e12)). **`RTI` therefore returns to the faulting instruction and re-executes it**, which is the whole mechanism: resolve the fault, return, and the access simply happens.
  NOTE: The CPU saves neither `A`, `B`, `X`, `Y`, `DB` nor `DP`, and changes no status bit but `D` and `I`. **The handler preserves them itself**, and that save set is the same audit [N.1](sec_n#n1) needs for the context switch.
  NOTE: Priority is `RESB` > `ABORTB` > `NMIB` > `IRQB`, and abort is **non-maskable**. An `NMIB` asserted at the same moment is taken after the abort handler returns.
- E.17 — **A write that has already driven the bus is not undone, and this is the single most important hardware rule in the design.** An abort discards *internal* results; a store cycle that reached the memory with `RWB` low has physically written. So copy-on-write is achievable only if the not-present or read-only decision is available **before the write strobe and gates it** — in the same cycle, in Helium:
  NOTE: **`WE_to_memory = RWB low AND cycle permitted AND NOT aborting this cycle`.** Wire it as one equation in one place. Every other protection on [sheet L](sec_l) is a permission bit; this one is the thing that makes a permission bit mean anything on a write.
- E.18 — **WDC's caveats enumerate four cases where an abort corrupts state, and they persist in the current datasheet.** Aborting a read-modify-write — `ASL`, `LSR`, `ROL`, `ROR`, `INC`, `DEC`, `TSB`, `TRB` — **after its modify cycle corrupts `P`, and the write to the target has already happened**, so a late abort leaves both a damaged flag register and a partial write. Aborting `RTI` after cycle 3 corrupts `P`. **Aborting after cycle 2 of any interrupt, `BRK`, `COP` or abort sequence forces `PBR` — and, in emulation mode, `DBR` — to `$00`.** And the datasheet is explicit that asynchronous aborts may therefore produce undesirable results.
  NOTE: **One rule covers all four: assert only on the memory cycle that generates the faulting address, at or before the instruction's last abortable cycle.** One cycle late is corruption. The third case is the one hardware must forbid rather than software avoid, and the guard is [E.15](sec_e#e15)'s: **Helium refuses to abort any cycle with `VPB` low**, which is exactly the window the abort, interrupt and `COP` sequences fetch their vectors in ([L.12](sec_l#l12) already watches that pin for privilege).
  NOTE: This is also the substance behind the community verdict that "ABORT is broken". The forum claim is an imprecise summary of the same cycle-level constraints; **where anecdote and the datasheet disagree, the datasheet's per-cycle statements are primary.**
- E.19 — **Restartable is not the same as precise, and only the first is needed.** *Precise* means the machine state at the fault is exactly the pre-instruction state; *restartable* means that after the handler fixes the fault, `RTI` re-runs the instruction and gets the right answer. The abort is **restartable for ordinary loads and stores when the write is gated and the fault is detected on the faulting cycle**, and **not precise** for the cases in [E.18](sec_e#e18). Demand paging needs restartability on the instructions that touch pageable memory — which is obtained by constraining *which* instructions may touch it ([E.21](sec_e#e21)), not by hoping.
- E.20 — **The abort sequence pushes to the stack, so an abort taken while the stack page is unmapped faults inside the fault and the machine deadlocks.** [L.11](sec_l#l11) already pins each process's stack pages for its lifetime, and [E.12](sec_e#e12) already notes the pushes. **This section makes the set explicit and makes it the kernel's job to enforce**: the stack pages, the direct page, the vectors, the abort and interrupt handlers, and any page holding code that performs a read-modify-write or a block move against non-pinned data — all `PIN=1`, and the kernel **refuses to unmap them** rather than merely declining to choose them as victims ([L.5](sec_l#l5), [L.13](sec_l#l13)).
- E.21 — **`MVN` and `MVP` are forbidden against pageable memory, and the ban is categorical rather than careful.** The block moves are designed to be *interruptible* — they decrement the accumulator and step the index registers per byte, so an interrupt resumes them — but that is exactly what makes them hostile here: a move straddles many pages, its per-byte state is mid-instruction, and [E.18](sec_e#e18)'s corruption windows sit inside it. Large copies go through a software loop of ordinary loads and stores, each individually restartable, or through Helium's own engines.
  NOTE: The rest of the discipline follows the same instinct and belongs in the toolchain rather than in the hardware ([sheet O](sec_o)). **Prefer load → modify in a register → store over a read-modify-write on pageable data**, so a fault lands on a plain access rather than on a modify cycle; where a read-modify-write on pageable data is unavoidable, **pin the target or pre-touch it with a probe load first**. Keep the stack and direct page in pinned physical RAM, which [L.11](sec_l#l11) already requires. And consider a **probe at function entry for large stack frames**, so the fault happens on a benign load rather than mid-instruction.
- E.22 — **`ABORTB` cannot stop an unprivileged task executing `WAI` or `STP`, and that is a privilege gap rather than a paging one.** Either untrusted code is not run, or Helium watches the instruction stream and aborts those opcodes at fetch — which is the one documented production use of the pin in another machine, where an FPGA traps opcodes it wants to emulate. **The cheap answer for v1 is a watchdog** ([D.7](sec_d#d7) already holds one), since a halted CPU is indistinguishable from a hung one from outside (→ [Q115](sec_q#q115)).

## Known ABORT limitations — the audit in one table.

| Limitation | Affected | Severity | What this design does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Abort after the modify cycle corrupts `P`, **and the write has already committed** | `ASL` `LSR` `ROL` `ROR` `INC` `DEC` `TSB` `TRB` | High | Assert only on the faulting cycle; gate `WE`; avoid read-modify-write on pageable data or pin the target ([E.21](sec_e#e21)) |
| Abort after cycle 3 corrupts `P` | `RTI` | Medium | Stack pinned ([E.20](sec_e#e20)); the abort logic never re-fires during the return |
| Abort after cycle 2 forces `PBR`/`DBR` to `$00` — aborting the abort | `IRQ` `NMI` `ABORT` `BRK` `COP` sequences | High | **Helium inhibits abort while `VPB` is low** ([E.18](sec_e#e18)) |
| A push during the abort sequence can itself fault → deadlock | Any abort with the stack page unmapped | **Critical** | Stack, direct page, vectors and handlers permanently pinned, and unmapping them refused ([E.20](sec_e#e20)) |
| Committed bus writes are never rolled back | Any faulting store | **Critical** | The write-gate equation of [E.17](sec_e#e17) |
| Mid-instruction register state across many pages | `MVN` `MVP` | High | Forbidden on pageable memory ([E.21](sec_e#e21)) |
| `WAI`/`STP` by unprivileged code cannot be trapped | `WAI` `STP` | Medium | Watchdog, or opcode detection at fetch ([E.22](sec_e#e22)) |
| The in-cycle decision is tight at the top of the speed range | every fault | Medium | 8 MHz gives 125 ns ([E.14](sec_e#e14)); a registered TLB if that ever changes |
| The 65C802 has an abort *vector* but no abort *pin* | wrong part | — | W65C816S only, as [E.1](sec_e#e1) specifies |

- E.23 — **Nobody has fielded this, and that is the honest headline.** The Apple IIGS — the highest-volume 65816 machine — does not use the pin at all; its chipset does bank switching, shadowing and speed matching, and every OS on it relies on banking rather than paging. The SNES's CPU is a licensed core plus a DMA-capable memory controller, and abort is not exposed for demand paging. The CMD SuperCPU and the Foenix machines use slot-based bank switching with no paging. **The strongest real deployment of the pin is instruction trapping**, in an FPGA-based 65816 machine that raises abort to catch banned opcodes and emulate them in software — which confirms the pin works, for a different purpose. Everything else is datasheet reading and small experiments; the community's own summary is that the pin is largely unexplored in a practical system.
  NOTE: Which is an argument for the bench work below rather than against the design. **The mechanism this machine needs is narrower than the one nobody has proved**: restartable aborts on plain loads and plain stores, with writes gated, and everything else forbidden by construction.
- E.24 — **Three staged bench experiments, and the first is a go/no-go before the OS design is committed.** With the board up, the TLB present and virtual memory disabled, put a logic analyser on `PHI2`, `VDA`, `VPA`, `RWB`, `ABORTB` and `VPB`, assert abort on a chosen load, and confirm **the correct address is stacked, `RTI` re-executes it, and `A`, `X`, `Y`, `P`, `DB` and `DP` are intact**. Repeat for a store and confirm the write was gated — memory unchanged. **If plain load and store aborts are clean and writes are suppressed, proceed; if not, the fallback is a stall-only design with statically mapped banks**, and it is better to know that before [P3.k](sec_p#p3k) than after ([Q116](sec_q#q116)).
  NOTE: **Then deliberately do the wrong thing, once, on purpose.** Assert abort one cycle late on a read-modify-write, on `RTI` after cycle 3, and after cycle 2 of an interrupt, and observe the corruption first-hand — to prove that the production logic cannot reach those cases rather than to believe that it does not. Then confirm the `VPB` inhibit holds under a fault taken during a vector pull.
  NOTE: The questions only silicon can answer are worth listing where they will be found: whether aborts on plain accesses are precise **100 %** of the time on the actual parts; whether the write gate holds across temperature at the target clock with no partial or glitch write; **the exact last abortable cycle for each addressing mode**, especially the indexed long modes and the page-crossing case that takes an extra cycle; and whether second-source parts, if one is ever substituted, behave identically ([E.1](sec_e#e1) specifies a WDC part for reasons that now include this one).

## Candidate cores — should the slot ever be populated.

| Core | Nature | Standing |
|---|---|---|
| P65816 (srg320) | VHDL, microcoded, cycle-exact | The most complete open option, and the reference this project already names. Basis of the MiSTer SNES core and the Apple IIgs core derived from it. Would be cleaned up and given a flat memory interface. |
| MiSTer Apple IIgs | Adaptation of srg320 | Validated in hardware at selectable speeds to 14.3 MHz. That is a *system compatibility* limit, not the isolated core's Fmax — it says nothing about what the core could reach. |
| FT816 | Accelerator, not cycle-exact | Conceived for speed rather than fidelity, so a better base where cycle exactness does not matter. Needs an instruction-coverage audit before it can be taken seriously. |
| WDC soft IP | Commercial | Paid, and against the project's open-toolchain principle ([A.6](sec_a#a6)). |
