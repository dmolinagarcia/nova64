# The expansion bus
> a slot that costs three pins · a slow bus that costs none · what a card may never assume

The machine grows in two directions and they are not the same problem. A **parallel slot** gives a card the machine's own bus — addresses, data, the clock and a validated window — and it is specified here as a second instance of a contract that already exists rather than as a new bus. A **slow bus** carries everything that never needed the bus at all, as a set of I2C devices mirrored into I/O windows. Neither is populated in the first build; both constrain the schematic, and one of them constrains a pin budget that is already short.

- X.1 — **The currency is pins on Helium, not pins on a connector**, and that single fact shapes every decision below. [Q8](sec_q#q8) is still blocking: seventeen free pins that already fail to cover SPI-SD, the UART console and the debug port. A proposal that spends ten is not a proposal. So the target this sheet sets itself is **three pins for the first slot, one for each slot after it, and zero for the slow bus** — the last because its two pins have been sitting in the budget since before the I2C bus split gave them nothing to do.
  NOTE: [D.6](sec_d#d6) closes with a prediction — *"both Helium and the RP2040 ran out of pins, and both were saved by moving slow signals onto a cheaper transport. Expect it a third time."* This sheet is the third time, and it is the reason the two tiers are specified together rather than in separate sheets: the slow bus exists so that the parallel slot never has to justify itself for a temperature sensor.
  NOTE: The three pins are spent at **layout**, not at population. An unpopulated connector still needs its nets routed to Helium, which is why [X.30](sec_x#x30) puts this decision before the board rather than before the first card.

## The slot — one more instance of the contract

- X.2 — **The slot is not a new bus. It is [B.6](sec_b#b6)'s three-net contract with a second tenant**, and the naming of those nets already anticipated it: [Q56](sec_q#q56) chose `NEON_BUS_SEL` over `VRAM_SEL` precisely because the net means *"Helium has validated this cycle for you"* and nothing narrower. A card gets the same three nets, the same stall protocol, the same watchdog and the same "Helium decodes the window, the tenant decodes inside it" split. **A slot that respects this table is a second Neon; one that does not is a second bus, and the board cannot afford a second bus.**
  NOTE: It also settles what a second slot is — another tenant of the same contract, never a second master: only the card whose `/EXP_SEL_n` is asserted may drive anything.

| Net | Direction | Asserted means | Idle / pulled state |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/EXP_SEL_n` | Helium → slot *n* | "This cycle is yours" — after the bank decode and the privilege check for a register cycle, after translation and the permission check for an aperture cycle ([X.7](sec_x#x7), [X.8](sec_x#x8)) | Deasserted. Push-pull, one driver, pull-up for the unconfigured case ([B.8](sec_b#b8)) |
| `/EXP_BSY` | slot → Helium, **common**, open-drain | "I cannot serve this cycle yet." Driven only by the card whose `/EXP_SEL_n` is asserted; released before any other slot can be selected | **Not busy.** Pull-up to 3V3_MAIN — the opposite of `NEON_BUS_BSY`, and [X.11](sec_x#x11) is why |
| `/EXP_IRQ` | slot → Helium's PIC, **common**, open-drain | "Somebody in a slot wants attention", demultiplexed by reading each slot's `SLOT_STATUS` ([X.13](sec_x#x13)) | Deasserted |

- X.3 — **What it costs, counted honestly.** `/EXP_SEL_n` is one pin per slot; `/EXP_BSY` and `/EXP_IRQ` are one pin each however many slots there are. Everything else on the connector — A0–A23, D0–D7, `RWB`, `PHI2`, the 25 MHz reference, `RESB`, 3V3 and ground — is a net that already exists on the board and reaches the connector through discrete logic, at **no cost in FPGA pins at all**. One slot is three pins, which is exactly what Neon costs; two slots are four. **How many slots the board carries is therefore the only variable in this sheet that fights [Q8](sec_q#q8)**, and it is left open deliberately (→ [Q81](sec_q#q81)).
  NOTE: A second slot is the cheapest thing on this list, and the 25 MHz reference is the only part of it that is not free: [B.7](sec_b#b7)'s star already says a fan-out buffer is the answer to a second destination rather than a fifth branch hung on the same net ([X.15](sec_x#x15)).

- X.4 — **The board demultiplexes the bank byte, and no card ever learns that the 65816 multiplexes it.** [F.3](sec_f#f3) latches the bank inside Helium and [T.51](sec_t#t51) does the same inside Neon, both of them saving eight pins by it — but making a third-party card capture D0–D7 during the low phase of PHI2 is making the author of a serial port understand the CPU's half-cycle pipeline. **One '573 on the motherboard, latching D0–D7 on the rising edge of PHI2**, and the connector presents A0–A23 static for the whole cycle. One package, zero FPGA pins.
  NOTE: It also pays for itself a second time, in [X.7](sec_x#x7) and [X.8](sec_x#x8): a card with the latched bank in front of it can tell a register cycle from an aperture cycle by itself — bank `$FF` or not — so the two address spaces need no signalling pin to separate them.
  NOTE: The '573 is also the one part of this sheet that has to exist before any card does, and it is not free of consequence: it latches from the same D0–D7 the CPU and both FPGAs already load ([F.4](sec_f#f4)), so it is one more load on the most layout-critical net on the board. That is the argument [X.5](sec_x#x5) then makes for buffering everything else.

- X.5 — **The slot is buffered, and this is not optional.** [F.4](sec_f#f4) calls D0–D7 and A0–A10 the most layout-critical nets on the board, with three loads already on them and an SRAM that must deliver inside a ~62 ns high phase. Hanging a connector, its stub and an unknown card off those nets is the cheapest way to lose that margin. So: a **'245** on the data lines, direction from `RWB`, **enabled only while some `/EXP_SEL_n` is asserted**; a **'244** on the address lines the '573 does not already buffer. The slot is electrically absent for the all traffic that is not addressed to it.
  NOTE: One rule inherited from [T.18](sec_t#t18) governs the transceiver and it is the one that will be got wrong: **nothing may drive the data bus toward the CPU while PHI2 is low**, because the bank byte is travelling on it. The '245's output enable is qualified by PHI2 high, not by `/EXP_SEL_n` alone.

- X.6 — **A card is reachable in two spaces, and the split is the same one [T.21](sec_t#t21) draws for Neon**: control in bank `$FF`, where the MMU can never take a process, and bulk through a window the MMU *can* map with permissions in front of it. Neither is a novelty; both are a comparator widened.

- X.7 — **Space one: a 256-byte register window per slot in bank `$FF`.** This is the block Helium deliberately does not decode: it recognises the window, asserts `/EXP_SEL_n`, releases D0–D7 on a read and performs the privilege check — and **the card decodes A0–A7 inside it**, exactly as Neon owns everything above `$FF:8000`. It inherits the keystone of [L.10](sec_l#l10) for free: bank `$FF` is unmappable to user space, so **no card in any slot is reachable from user code by construction**, whatever the card is and whoever wrote it. The proposal, on [Q22](sec_q#q22)'s terms of one 256-byte wall per block, is `$FF:7E00` for slot 0 and `$FF:7F00` for slot 1 — the top of Helium's half, against Neon at the bottom of the other one (→ [Q82](sec_q#q82)).
  NOTE: In a register cycle Helium performs no translation ([F.3](sec_f#f3)), so **A11–A19 carry nothing** and a card must not decode them. The registers are byte-wide and byte-addressable, unlike [M.4](sec_m#m4)'s block: a card is an eight-bit device on an eight-bit bus and there is no reason to make a serial port pretend otherwise.

- X.8 — **Space two: a 1 MB aperture per slot, mapped by the MMU, at zero pin cost — and this is the part that is not obvious.** [F.10](sec_f#f10) sets the ceiling at 128 MB and populates half of it, so **the upper half of the frame-number space decodes to nothing**. Give a range of it to the slots: a PTE naming a frame there is a legal mapping that reaches a card instead of SDRAM, with the TLB, the permission bits and `ABORTB` still in front of it. And the address gets there on wires that are already driven: during a slot cycle the SRAM's `/CE` is idle, so **A11–A19 — which Helium drives anyway ([F.2](sec_f#f2)) — carry the offset within the aperture**, and A0–A10 come from the CPU as they always do. Twenty bits, 1 MB per slot, not one new pin.
  NOTE: The proposal is frames `$F000`–`$F1FF` for slot 0 and `$F200`–`$F3FF` for slot 1 — 512 frames of 2 KB each. The range is arbitrary and the *size* is not: it is what A0–A19 reaches, and widening it means new pins (→ [Q83](sec_q#q83)).
  NOTE: What this buys is the thing a register window cannot: **a card's memory can be mapped into a process with protection**. A frame buffer, a packet ring, a sample buffer — reachable at bus speed by user code that still cannot escape its own address space, and still cannot reach the card's control registers, because those are in `$FF`. It is [J.5](sec_j#j5)'s framebuffer mapping generalised to devices nobody has designed yet.

- X.9 — **Aperture frames are non-cacheable, always, and the gateware must enforce it rather than trust the flag.** The `NC` bit exists ([M.9](sec_m#m9)) and the kernel must set it — but a cached aperture is not a performance question, it is a correctness one: a cache line is 2 KB of a device's registers held in SRAM and written back whenever the cache feels like it. Helium must treat a frame number in the expansion range as `NC` regardless of what the PTE says, and a fill must never be started against one.

- X.10 — **The read protocol is [T.18](sec_t#t18)'s, unchanged: Helium stalls first and asks afterwards.** Helium knows a slot cycle is in progress at the same instant the card does, since it decoded it. So it asserts `/EXP_SEL_n`, gates PHI2 low, and only *then* samples `/EXP_BSY`. A card that answers inside the high phase — a register file, a FIFO — never asserts it and costs nothing; a card that needs longer holds it and the CPU is frozen, not misinformed. As with Neon, **the clock is gated and `RDY` is not touched**, for [D16](sec_q#d16)'s reason.
  NOTE: Sampling starts after a mandatory minimum window of two core clocks rather than immediately, and that window is the whole of what makes [X.11](sec_x#x11) safe.

- X.11 — **`/EXP_BSY` idles *not busy*, which inverts Neon's polarity, and the inversion is the point.** [B.6](sec_b#b6) makes `NEON_BUS_BSY` busy-by-default so that an unfitted Neon stalls the machine and the watchdog reports it — correct for a soldered device that is either there or is a bring-up stage. **An empty slot is neither**: busy-by-default in a slot means a machine with nothing plugged in does not boot. The race that argument was defending against — Helium sampling "not busy" before the card has noticed the cycle — is killed by the mandatory stall window of [X.10](sec_x#x10) instead of by the pull-up, which is a better place for it: the guarantee becomes a property of the protocol rather than of a resistor.
  NOTE: The safety property is not lost, only moved. **An empty slot answers with the pull-ups and an absent card is indistinguishable from a broken one — until enumeration**, where `SLOT_ID` reading `$00` says "nothing there" in one register access ([X.14](sec_x#x14)).

- X.12 — **The fill watchdog gains a third client.** [F.11](sec_f#f11)'s counter already resumes PHI2 and raises NMI or `ABORT` for an overrunning cache fill, and [T.18](sec_t#t18) gave it a second trigger in a Neon that never answers. A card that asserts `/EXP_BSY` and dies is exactly the same failure with a third source: **resume the clock, return `$00`, raise the fault**, and give it its own `FAULT_CAUSE` code so the kernel can say *which slot* rather than *something timed out*. The timeout value is [Q57](sec_q#q57)'s and is still unassigned; this makes it one number serving three clients, which is an argument for settling it rather than against.
  NOTE: Three clients, one escalation path — an overrunning fill, a silent Neon, a card holding `/EXP_BSY`. A hung machine reports the same way whatever hung it.

- X.13 — **One interrupt line for every slot, demultiplexed by a mandatory register — because that is what Neon already does.** A line per slot into the PIC gives exact vectoring and costs a pin per slot, which [X.3](sec_x#x3) will not pay. [T.47](sec_t#t47) aggregates three sources inside Neon and has the kernel read `IRQ_STATUS`; the slot does the same across devices instead of within one. What makes it cheap rather than ISA-shaped is that the demultiplexing offset is **normative, not negotiated**: bit 7 of `SLOT_STATUS` at a fixed offset in every card's window, so an ISR costs one read per populated slot and the answer is deterministic.
  NOTE: `/EXP_IRQ` is **level-sensitive and open-drain**, so two cards asserting simultaneously is an ordinary wired-OR rather than a lost edge, and the handler must loop until no slot claims it.

- X.14 — **Enumeration costs no pins either.** [M.4](sec_m#m4) already rules that an address nothing answers reads `$00`, so an empty slot identifies itself: **`SLOT_ID` reading `$00` means the slot is empty**, and no card-present pin is needed. It is [T.54](sec_t#t54)'s `NEON_ID == $4E` generalised — the BIOS reads it once, expects a card or does not, and continues either way. A card learns **which slot it is in** from two connector pins strapped differently in each position, so the same board works in either slot with no jumper; those straps are board traces to 3V3 and ground and cost nothing anywhere.

- X.15 — **PHI2 goes to the connector, and the first thing a card author must be told is that it is not a clock.** Helium gates it — for cache fills ([F.4](sec_f#f4)), for Neon reads ([T.18](sec_t#t18)), and now for the slot itself. A card that derives a baud rate, a sample rate or a timeout from PHI2 will drift by an amount that depends on what the rest of the machine is doing, which is the least debuggable class of fault this board can produce. **`PHI2` is a bus qualifier; the 25 MHz reference is the time base.** That reference is a branch of [B.7](sec_b#b7)'s single-oscillator star with its own 33 Ω in series, and a second slot means a fan-out buffer rather than a fifth branch on the same net.

- X.16 — **A card is never a bus master in this revision, and the reason is coherence rather than arbitration.** [M.12](sec_m#m12) states the rule that governs any master writing memory behind the cache: invalidate before a read into memory, flush before a write out of it, and each mistake is a silent corruption that only appears under memory pressure. Helium's own SD engine can honour it because Helium owns the cache; **a third-party card cannot honour it and cannot be made to.** DMA therefore stays a privilege of the northbridge. `/EXP_BR` and `/EXP_BG` are routed to the connector and left unconnected at the FPGA, in the same spirit as [F.10](sec_f#f10)'s second SDRAM `/CS`: the board does not foreclose the decision, and this revision does not take it.

- X.17 — **Reset, insertion and the blank-bitstream case.** `RESB` reaches the connector buffered, so a card comes up with the CPU. **There is no hot-plug**: cards are inserted with the machine off, which deletes sequencing, inrush and connector-order questions that would otherwise each need an answer. And a card must be safe with a blank Helium — every slot control net is pulled to its idle state by [B.8](sec_b#b8)'s rule, so an unconfigured board selects nothing and a card that drives the bus only when selected drives nothing.

- X.18 — **A card is testable before a kernel exists, and this falls out of [M.10](sec_m#m10) at no cost.** The debug agent's internal accesses land on the same address comparators a CPU store does, so the RP2040 can read a card's `SLOT_ID`, poke its registers and watch its interrupt with the 65816 held in reset and no software anywhere. For a subsystem whose whole purpose is to host hardware this document did not design, that is worth more than it is for any of the blocks that already have it.
  NOTE: So a card is brought up with the 65816 socket still empty, exactly as Neon is.

- X.19 — **Connector: a 2 × 26 header on 2.54 mm pitch**, which is the project's hand-solderable rule applied to a part that also has to survive being a mechanical interface. Signal count below. Ground is alternated along one row rather than gathered at the ends: at 8 MHz the risk is not the frequency, it is the return path under a 25 MHz clock edge and a bus turnaround.

| Group | Pins | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| A0–A23 | 24 | A16–A23 from the board's '573 ([X.4](sec_x#x4)); A11–A19 meaningful only in an aperture cycle |
| D0–D7 | 8 | Through the '245, never driven toward the CPU while PHI2 is low |
| `RWB` · `RESB` | 2 | Buffered |
| `PHI2` · `CLK25` | 2 | A qualifier and a time base — not interchangeable ([X.15](sec_x#x15)) |
| `/EXP_SEL_n` · `/EXP_BSY` · `/EXP_IRQ` | 3 | The contract of [X.2](sec_x#x2) |
| `SDA` · `SCL` | 2 | The slow bus, so that a card can be a slow device without touching any of the above ([X.27](sec_x#x27)) |
| `SLOTID0` · `SLOTID1` | 2 | Geographic strapping, board traces ([X.14](sec_x#x14)) |
| `/EXP_BR` · `/EXP_BG` | 2 | Routed, not connected at the FPGA ([X.16](sec_x#x16)) |
| 3V3 · GND | 3 · 4 | |

- X.20 — **The card contract, in one place, because a slot is a promise made to people who will not read the rest of this document.** A card may assume: static A0–A23, `RWB` and a full PHI2 high phase to answer in; that it is selected only when the cycle is genuinely its own; that it will never be selected by an unconfigured board. A card must: drive D0–D7 only while selected, on a read, during PHI2 high; release `/EXP_BSY` before deasserting; implement `SLOT_ID`, `SLOT_CLASS` and `SLOT_STATUS` at the offsets below; hold `/EXP_IRQ` no longer than its own status bit. A card must never: master the bus; use PHI2 as a time base; decode A11–A19 in a register cycle; decode bank `$FF` in an aperture cycle; hold `/EXP_BSY` longer than [Q57](sec_q#q57)'s window.
  NOTE: Most of that list is enforced by the board rather than trusted to the card: the select line, the '245's direction and the watchdog do it. Only the decode rules depend on the card, and getting them wrong misbehaves inside its own window.

## Register map — the slot window. Offsets from `$FF:7E00` (slot 0) and `$FF:7F00` (slot 1), still to be ratified (→ [Q82](sec_q#q82)). Byte-wide.

| Offset | Name | Access | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `+$00`–`+$EF` | — | — | The card's own. Helium decodes nothing here and reads `$00` when no card answers |
| `+$F0` | SLOT_STATUS | read | Bit 7 `IRQ_PENDING` — **normative, this is what the ISR demultiplexes on** ([X.13](sec_x#x13)) · 6:0 the card's |
| `+$F2` | SLOT_CTRL | write | Bit 0 IRQ enable · 1 clear `IRQ_PENDING` · 7:2 the card's |
| `+$FC` | SLOT_CLASS | read | What kind of device this is. Class numbering unallocated (→ [Q84](sec_q#q84)) |
| `+$FE` | SLOT_ID | read | Non-zero identifies the card; **`$00` means the slot is empty** ([X.14](sec_x#x14)) |

## The slow bus — I2C devices as I/O windows

- X.21 — **The second tier exists so the first one is never asked to carry a temperature sensor.** An RTC, an expander, a sensor, an EEPROM: none of them needs an address bus, a stall protocol or a megabyte of aperture, and putting them behind a parallel slot means a connector and a buffer per trivial device. [D.6](sec_d#d6) already solved this problem twice on this board — the keyboard matrix onto USB HID, the rail enables onto an I2C expander — and both times the pattern was *move the slow signals onto a cheaper transport*.
  NOTE: The tier has a floor: a device with a deadline does not belong on it. A byte is ~90 µs ([X.23](sec_x#x23)), so anything that must answer inside a bus cycle or a frame belongs on the slot or inside Helium — this tier is for state that changes at human rates.

- X.22 — **It is a third I2C bus, Helium's own, and it must not be either of the existing two.** [C.13](sec_c#c13)'s rule R3 split I2C in two for a reason that still holds: **I2C-AON** belongs to the charger and the gauge, and every device added to it costs standby current for the life of a charge; **I2C-SW** is the EC's, and [E8.1](sec_p#e81) has the EC reading the GT911 on it. Making Helium a master on either means genuine multi-master arbitration in gateware, against [B.2](sec_b#b2)'s rule that Helium is a pure state machine — and it would collide head-on with [Q4](sec_q#q4)'s proposal that the HID path run with the EC as master and Helium as *slave*. So: **`I2C-EXP`, two pins, Helium the only master, pulls to 3V3_MAIN under rule R2, in the switched domain because Helium is.** The pins are not new — [Q8](sec_q#q8) has been carrying "I2C 2" in Helium's allocation since before the split left them without a device.
  NOTE: **This gives [Q35](sec_q#q35) an answer it did not have.** The PCF8563 was left unassigned because it wants to keep time while the machine is off, which argues the always-on bus, against a bus reserved to the charger and the gauge. On `I2C-EXP` with its own backup cell it keeps time with no bus at all — the crystal does not care who is powered — and the kernel then reads the clock directly instead of asking the EC for it. It becomes window 0 and the first tenant of this tier.

- X.23 — **A window is a mirror, not a proxy, and the arithmetic is what forces that.** The tempting design has a CPU read of a window perform the I2C transaction — but a byte over I2C is nine bit-times plus addressing: **~90 µs at 100 kHz, ~23 µs at 400 kHz**, against a 125 ns bus cycle. Serving it by stalling PHI2 violates [M.6](sec_m#m6) by three orders of magnitude, and there is no version of "the kernel polls instead" that survives being spelled `lda $FF7C04`. So the window **never touches the wire**: it is a copy in Helium's block RAM, refreshed by a sequencer, and a CPU read of it is a register read at full bus speed like any other.

- X.24 — **The refresh mechanism already exists: it is [S.16](sec_s#s16)'s, with a device table in front of it.** Sheet S solved exactly this problem for one device — a slow instrument, an asynchronous updater, an eight-bit CPU that must not see a multi-byte value tear — and solved it with **staging → live → shadow**: the slow side writes staging in any order, a commit copies it to live in one cycle, the CPU latches live into a shadow bank and reads the shadow as slowly as it likes. Here the slow side is the I2C sequencer rather than the EC, the device is whatever the descriptor names, and everything else is the same machinery. **The atomicity and staleness properties come along unchanged**, which is the whole argument for this shape over a transaction register: one mechanism, already specified, already justified, now serving *n* devices instead of one.
  NOTE: Per-window refresh period rather than one global rate — an RTC at 1 Hz and a sensor at 10 Hz have nothing to say to each other, and [S.20](sec_s#s20) already makes the point that cadence costs power on the slow side.

- X.25 — **Writes are queued, never immediate, and the completion story is the one the rest of the document uses.** A CPU write into a window lands in the shadow and sets a dirty bit; the sequencer carries it to the device on its next pass. A `WIN_FLUSH` trigger forces the pass for the case where ordering matters. Completion is a status bit and, optionally, the same aggregated interrupt the slots use — because a driver that has to wait 90 µs for an acknowledgement should be blocked on an interrupt, exactly as [G.10](sec_g#g10) argues for the SD engine.
  NOTE: Ordering *between* windows is not defined: the sequencer visits descriptors on its own schedule, so writes to two devices land in whatever order the pass reaches them. Where order matters the driver serialises with `WIN_FLUSH`, never with timing.

- X.26 — **A device that stops answering must read as a fault, not as a plausible zero** — [S.19](sec_s#s19)'s rule, and it is the one thing about this tier that is not negotiable. Every window carries a `VALID` bit, an age, and a NAK/bus-error flag; a window whose device has gone silent stops updating and says so, rather than mirroring the last good value forever. A driver that ignores the flag reads a battery frozen at whatever it was when the wire fell off.
  NOTE: The rule has a kernel-side half: a driver reads `VALID` and the age *before* the data, and a window in fault is reported upward, not smoothed over. [S.19](sec_s#s19) states it for the battery, where a frozen but plausible value is the most dangerous reading there is.

- X.27 — **`SDA` and `SCL` are on the slot connector as well, and that makes the cheapest possible card.** A board that only wants an identity, a couple of GPIOs or a sensor implements no bus protocol at all: it is an I2C device, it gets a window, and it never asserts `/EXP_BSY` because it never sees a cycle. It is also the natural home for a per-card identity EEPROM, if [X.14](sec_x#x14)'s `SLOT_ID` ever proves too thin.
  NOTE: Such a card needs no buffer, no '245 and no select line — two wires.

- X.28 — **What this tier deliberately does not specify yet**, and the honest list is short because the mechanism is borrowed rather than invented: how many windows, how large each is, the descriptor encoding, the sequencer's scheduling policy when several windows come due at once, and whether the raw-transaction escape hatch of [X.29](sec_x#x29) is enough to initialise every device the tier is likely to host. The register map below is a proposal at that resolution and nothing more (→ [Q85](sec_q#q85)).
  NOTE: None of those five gaps blocks the only decision this sheet must settle before layout — the pins ([X.30](sec_x#x30)). They block the first driver, and the first driver does not exist until a device does.

- X.29 — **Device initialisation stays in software, behind a raw-transaction register**, for [G.11](sec_g#g11)'s reason applied one tier down: Helium's gateware does the shifting and the sequencing, and nothing above that. A device needing a setup sequence — a mode register, a resolution, an alert threshold — gets it through `I2C_RAW_*` from the kernel driver, and only then is its window enabled and mirrored. This is [B.2](sec_b#b2)'s rule about where intelligence lives, applied to the tier most tempting to violate it for.
  NOTE: The escape hatch doubles as the audit: anything a driver can do *only* through `I2C_RAW_*` is a candidate for a descriptor field that does not exist yet, and the list of those cases is the evidence [X.28](sec_x#x28) is waiting for.

## Register map — the slow-bus block. **Proposal, not ratified** ([[open]] → [Q85](sec_q#q85)). One 256-byte wall, base unassigned ([Q22](sec_q#q22)). Byte-wide.

| Offset | Name | Access | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `+$00` | I2C_CTRL | write | Bit 0 sequencer enable · 1 bus speed (100/400 kHz) · 2 bus reset · 3 IRQ enable |
| `+$01` | I2C_STATUS | read | Bit 0 sequencer running · 1 raw transaction busy · 2 any window in fault · 3 IRQ pending |
| `+$02` | I2C_SEQ | read | Bumped on every completed refresh pass — the staleness reference of [S.16](sec_s#s16) |
| `+$03` | I2C_LATCH | write | Copy live → shadow in one cycle. Read the shadow afterwards, tear-free |
| `+$08`–`+$0F` | I2C_RAW_* | read/write | Device address, register, length, data and trigger for an unmirrored transaction ([X.29](sec_x#x29)) |
| `+$10`+ | WIN*n*_DEV · _BASE · _LEN · _PERIOD · _STAT | read/write | One descriptor per window: 7-bit device address and flags, first device register mirrored, byte count, refresh period, and `VALID`/age/NAK |
| `+$80`+ | Window shadow data | read/write | The mirrors themselves. A read is a register read; a write sets the dirty bit of [X.25](sec_x#x25) |

- X.30 — **None of this is on the critical path, and saying so is part of the specification.** The slot has no tenant, the slow bus has one candidate, and neither is required by any milestone: [E6](sec_p#e6) boots without them and [E8](sec_p#e8) is a laptop without them. What they require *now* is schematic space and three or four pins reserved before layout, because both are cheap to route and impossible to add afterwards — and that is exactly the class of decision [E1.4](sec_p#e14)'s staged population was designed to protect. Where the gateware lands in the E-series is open, and it should land late (→ [Q86](sec_q#q86)).
  NOTE: The dates say the same thing. The slot's cost falls at [E1.1](sec_p#e11), when the schematic sheets are captured; its gateware is not owed until something is plugged in. Reserving routing is cheap, and a respin to add a connector is not.
